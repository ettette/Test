Land acknowledgement

Honoring Native peoples, homelands, and continued presence.

We offer this acknowledgement with respect for Indigenous sovereignty, culture, knowledge, and stewardship across California.

Acknowledgement

The land we are connected to

Indigenous Future Coalition is based in the Bay Area, on the ancestral and unceded homelands of Ohlone peoples. We acknowledge the Ramaytush Ohlone, Muwekma Ohlone Tribe, Tamien Nation, Amah Mutsun Tribal Band, and other Ohlone communities whose relationships with these lands continue today.

We also recognize that California is home to many Native nations, communities, and peoples. These include, among many others, Pomo, Miwok, Yokuts, Wintun, Maidu, Nisenan, Chumash, Tongva, Tataviam, Acjachemen, Kumeyaay, Cahuilla, Serrano, Luiseño, Paiute, Shoshone, Karuk, Yurok, Hupa, Wiyot, Tolowa Dee-ni', and Modoc peoples. No written acknowledgement can fully name every community or reflect the depth of each people's history, language, governance, and relationship to place.

We acknowledge that Native peoples in California have endured colonization, forced removal, missionization, violence, broken agreements, suppression of culture and language, and policies that tried to separate Native communities from their lands and each other. We also recognize that Native peoples are not only part of the past. Tribal nations, Native families, elders, youth, educators, artists, culture bearers, and community leaders continue to sustain languages, traditions, knowledge systems, environmental stewardship, and sovereign governance.

As a student-led organization, we understand that a land acknowledgement should be more than a statement. It should remind us to listen first, respect community priorities, avoid speaking over Native voices, and build relationships with humility. Our work is guided by the belief that projects involving Native communities should be shaped by what those communities identify as useful, respectful, and needed.

Our responsibility

Listen to community direction

We aim to support projects that come from community-identified needs, goals, and priorities.

Respect sovereignty

We recognize Native nations as sovereign governments with their own leadership, protocols, knowledge, and decision-making.

Keep learning

We commit to learning from Native educators, organizations, histories, and contemporary voices rather than treating acknowledgement as a finished action.
