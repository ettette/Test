Indigenous holidays

Important days for learning, honoring, and showing respect.

These observances are starting points for reflection. Specific dates, names, and traditions can vary by Native nation, region, and community.

Calendar guide

Major Indigenous holidays and observances

Some of these days are official holidays in certain places, while others are awareness days or months. The most respectful way to take part is to learn from Native-led sources, attend public community events when invited, and support Indigenous organizations year-round.

Second Monday October

United States

Indigenous Peoples' Day

A day to honor Native peoples, histories, cultures, sovereignty, and continued presence. Many states, cities, schools, and organizations observe it on the second Monday in October.

All Month November

United States

Native American Heritage Month

A month dedicated to recognizing the histories, cultures, achievements, and contributions of Native American, Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian communities.

Friday After Thanksgiving

United States

Native American Heritage Day

A national observance that continues the focus of Native American Heritage Month and encourages learning, recognition, and respect.

Fourth Friday September

California

California Native American Day

A state observance honoring California Native peoples, tribal nations, cultures, languages, histories, and leadership.

May 5

United States and Canada

MMIP Awareness Day

A day of awareness for Missing and Murdered Indigenous Persons, often marked by wearing red, listening to families and advocates, and supporting community-led safety work.

June 21

Canada

National Indigenous Peoples Day

A day recognizing and celebrating the history, heritage, resilience, and diversity of First Nations, Inuit, and Métis peoples.

August 9

Global

International Day of the World's Indigenous Peoples

A United Nations observance focused on Indigenous peoples' rights, cultures, knowledge, and priorities around the world.

September 30

Canada

National Day for Truth and Reconciliation

A day to remember the history and ongoing impacts of residential schools and to honor Survivors, families, and communities.

Respectful participation

Ways to observe thoughtfully

Learn locally

Find out whose homelands you live on and look for public learning resources from local tribal nations or Native-led organizations.

Support Native-led work

Attend public events when invited, share Native-created resources, and support organizations led by Indigenous communities.

Keep learning after the date

These days matter most when they lead to ongoing respect, better relationships, and action beyond a single calendar moment.
